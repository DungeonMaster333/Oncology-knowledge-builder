## Oncology Concepts to Learn

1. **FDG-PET (Fludeoxyglucose Positron Emission Tomography):** 
   * *What it is:* A nuclear imaging technique that uses a radioactive sugar molecule (Fludeoxyglucose F-18).
   * *Why it matters in cancer:* Cancer cells grow and divide rapidly, consuming much higher levels of glucose than most normal tissues (a phenomenon called the *Warburg effect*). When FDG is injected, malignant lesions take up the tracer and "light up" on a PET scanner, allowing clinicians to stage tumors and monitor whether therapies are working.
2. **Radiopharmaceuticals:**
   * *What they are:* Medicinal formulations containing radioisotopes. They are used either for diagnostics (emitting signals detected by scanners, like Fluorine-18) or for targeted therapeutics (emitting targeted radiation to kill cancer cells, such as Lutetium-177).
3. **Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA):**
   * *What it is:* An FDA submission pathway for generic medicines. Instead of repeating costly preclinical and clinical trials, the applicant demonstrates that the generic drug performs in the same manner as the original brand-name reference drug.
4. **Keyword Sensitivity vs. Specificity in Oncology CI:**
   * *What it is:* Algorithms often flag records containing words like "tumor" (e.g., *Tumor Necrosis Factor* inhibitors like Cimzia, which are used for autoimmune conditions) or ancillary terms in product labels. Analysts must distinguish actual oncology indications from non-oncology drugs caught by broad keyword filters.
