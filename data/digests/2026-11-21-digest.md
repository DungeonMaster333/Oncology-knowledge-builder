  * **What it is:** Fludeoxyglucose F-18 (FDG) is a radioactive glucose analog used in Positron Emission Tomography (PET) imaging. 
  * **Clinical & Commercial Relevance:** Rapidly growing cancer cells consume high amounts of glucose (the Warburg effect). Because FDG accumulates in metabolically active cells, it allows clinicians to locate, stage, and track tumors and assess therapeutic response across dozens of cancer types. Due to the very short radioactive half-life of Fluorine-18 (~110 minutes), supply must be produced regionally or locally. Approval of an ANDA for a leading academic cancer center (MD Anderson) ensures secure, in-house supply for high-volume cancer staging and clinical trial imaging.

## Oncology Concepts to Learn

1. **Fludeoxyglucose F-18 (FDG) & PET Imaging:** A radiotracer used in Positron Emission Tomography. Because tumor cells generally exhibit higher glucose uptake than normal tissue, FDG acts as a beacon, allowing oncologists to visualize primary tumors and metastases.
2. **Radiopharmaceuticals (Diagnostics vs. Therapeutics):** Drugs containing radioactive isotopes. Diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals (like FDG F-18) emit positrons or gamma rays detected by scanners, whereas therapeutic radiopharmaceuticals emit alpha or beta particles to destroy cancer cells.
3. **Institutional ANDAs in Nuclear Medicine:** Unlike standard tablets or biologics, radiopharmaceuticals decay within hours. Academic cancer centers often register and hold their own Abbreviated New Drug Applications (ANDAs) to legally manufacture and distribute generic imaging agents on-site via cyclotrons.
4. **Keyword Screening vs. True Oncology Relevance:** Automated databases often capture drugs under "oncology" due to keyword matches (e.g., Cimzia matching "tumor" because it targets Tumor Necrosis Factor in autoimmune diseases). Analysts must differentiate direct oncology therapeutics from supportive care or unrelated indications.
