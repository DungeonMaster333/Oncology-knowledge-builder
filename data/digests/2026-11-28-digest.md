  * **Company / Applicant:** University of Texas MD Anderson Cancer Center
  * **Regulatory Action:** Original Approval (`ORIG · AP`), Abbreviated New Drug Application (`ANDA 203246`)
  * **Details & Form:** Injectable, Intravenous (strength: 20–150 mCi/mL); Prescription status.
  * **What it is (Simple Explanation):** Fludeoxyglucose F18 (FDG F-18) is a radioactive sugar molecule used as a diagnostic tracer in Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scans. Because cancer cells grow rapidly and consume much more glucose than normal cells, they absorb high amounts of FDG, making tumors "light up" on a PET scan.
  * **Clinical & Commercial Impact:** FDG-PET imaging is an essential diagnostic cornerstone across medical oncology. Clinically, it is required to detect primary tumors, stage nodal and distant metastases, and assess metabolic response to chemotherapy and immunotherapy. Commercially, radiopharmaceuticals have very short radioactive half-lives and cannot be stockpiled; institutional approvals (such as this ANDA from a leading cancer center like MD Anderson) ensure reliable, localized supply and cost-effective diagnostic access for oncology patients and clinical trial staging.

## Oncology Concepts to Learn

1. **FDG-PET Imaging (Fluorodeoxyglucose F-18 Positron Emission Tomography):** 
   A nuclear medicine imaging modality that maps glucose metabolism. In oncology, it is widely used alongside CT or MRI to detect tumors, map disease spread (staging), and check whether a tumor has stopped growing after therapy.
2. **The Warburg Effect:** 
   The biological rationale behind FDG imaging. Cancer cells predominantly produce energy through a high rate of glycolysis followed by lactic acid fermentation (even in the presence of oxygen), causing them to consume glucose at significantly higher rates than healthy tissue.
3. **ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application):** 
   An FDA regulatory submission pathway used for generic drug approvals. Rather than repeating costly preclinical and clinical safety/efficacy trials, an ANDA demonstrates that a generic product is bioequivalent and performs in the same manner as the original brand-name drug.
4. **Keyword Disambiguation in Competitive Intelligence:** 
   Biological terms often create false positives. For example, "Tumor Necrosis Factor" (TNF) includes the word *tumor*, but TNF inhibitors (such as Cimzia/certolizumab pegol) are primarily used to treat inflammatory and autoimmune diseases, not active cancers.
