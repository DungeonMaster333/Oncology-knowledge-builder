  * **Regulatory Action:** The FDA approved an original Abbreviated New Drug Application (ANDA203246) for Fludeoxyglucose F-18 injection (strength: 20–150 mCi/mL), an intravenous prescription radiopharmaceutical sponsored by University of Texas MD Anderson.
  * **Plain-Language Explanation:** Fludeoxyglucose F-18 (FDG) is a radioactive glucose tracer used in Positron Emission Tomography (PET) scans. Because malignant tumor cells consume glucose at a significantly higher rate than surrounding healthy tissues, injecting FDG illuminates active cancer lesions on a scan.
  * **Commercial & Clinical Relevance:** FDG-PET imaging is standard-of-care across solid tumors and hematologic malignancies for initial staging, restaging, and evaluating therapeutic response. Institutional approvals (such as MD Anderson's) ensure reliable tracer access for diagnostic workups and oncology clinical trial imaging workflows without relying solely on commercial radiopharmacies.

## Oncology Concepts to Learn

1. **FDG-PET Imaging & The Warburg Effect:** Cancer cells often rely on aerobic glycolysis (metabolizing glucose much faster than normal cells, known as the Warburg effect). Fludeoxyglucose F-18 leverages this mechanism to highlight active tumors during PET scans.
2. **Diagnostic Radiopharmaceuticals:** Unlike therapeutic oncology drugs designed to kill cancer cells, diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals emit detectable radiation (such as positrons) to visualize disease location, burden, and response to treatment.
3. **ANDA (Abbreviated New Drug Application):** The regulatory filing submitted to the FDA for generic drugs or established imaging agents, showing that the product matches the brand/reference drug in quality, strength, dosage form, and route of administration.
4. **CI Keyword Disambiguation (e.g., "Tumor" in Non-Oncology):** Keyword filters often trigger false positives. For instance, drugs targeting "Tumor Necrosis Factor" (like Cimzia) are primarily immunology drugs for inflammatory conditions (e.g., rheumatoid arthritis, Crohn's), not oncology therapeutics.
